01 The Insight — A Consequence Gap, Not a Knowledge Gap

Why financial literacy interventions consistently fail — and what ArthSaathi does differently.

India has 530 million UPI users, yet more than 75% of Indian adults do not adequately understand basic financial concepts (NCFE-FLIS 2019). The instinct is to solve this with more education. The evidence says that doesn't work.

Traditional financial education with delayed delivery — workshops, articles, courses consumed days or weeks before a financial decision — explains only 0.1% of variance in financial behavior (Fernandes, Lynch & Netemeyer, 2014, Management Science). This doesn't mean all financial guidance is useless. It means guidance disconnected from the moment of decision has near-zero behavioral effect. ArthSaathi's approach is different: make financial consequences emotionally vivid at the exact moment of decision, not days later in a classroom.

The Core Problem — Hot-Cold Empathy Gap

Loewenstein (2005) showed that people in "cold" states (calm, reflective) cannot predict their behavior in "hot" states (stressed, tempted, desperate). Rajesh knows predatory loans are bad. He takes one anyway because he's desperate. ArthSaathi's WhatsApp integration means the intervention happens on the same screen, in the same moment he receives the scam message — meeting him at the point of temptation, not hours later in a separate app.

02 ArthSaathi — Four Capabilities

Designed to protect, translate, simulate, and guide. Will never lecture, judge, or sell.
🛡 Protect
Sentinel
Designed to analyze financial offers, URLs, and messages that users actively forward via WhatsApp. No device monitoring required. Trust Score is computed deterministically using a weighted formula (RBI/SEBI registration, APR threshold, pattern match, community reports, domain age). The LLM explains the score; it never generates it. Explainable warnings surface at least 2 RBI-registered alternatives per threat.
📄 Translate
Drishti
Photograph any financial document — bank statement, insurance policy, loan agreement — to receive a "nutrition label": true cost in rupees (not percentages), color-coded risk level, better alternatives, and suitability for your situation. In your language. The LLM extracts fields; a deterministic Python engine computes all financial math. The LLM will never touch a calculation.
⏳ Simulate — The Core
Kalpa
Before any financial decision, users will experience the consequences. For fixed-return products (loans, FDs), the system will use deterministic amortization math. For variable-return products (SIPs, equity), projection scenarios at P25/P50/P75 will be derived from 10-year AMFI historical return data — pessimistic, expected, and optimistic paths shown side by side. Full Monte Carlo (10,000 iterations) is planned post-Grand Finale.
🚨 Guide
Prahari
Will operate on two data streams: (a) user-reported financial events (income changes, loan applications) entered via WhatsApp, and (b) uploaded bank statements processed by Drishti. Detects anomaly patterns such as loan-to-loan cycling or repeated scam exposure, and triggers proactive outreach. Account Aggregator integration for consent-based transaction monitoring is on the ROADMAP.
🔗 How They Work Together
The Simulation Is the Nervous System
Every capability feeds the simulation core. Sentinel detects a scam → Kalpa simulates "what if you'd fallen for it?" Drishti scans a ULIP → Kalpa simulates "ULIP vs. Term + SIP for 12 years." Prahari predicts a crisis → Kalpa simulates "with vs. without government scheme intervention." The simulation is not a standalone feature — it is the behavioral change engine that every other component activates. Rajesh forwards a predatory loan offer; within 60 seconds he sees his debt spiral to ₹67,000 and the MUDRA alternative saving him ₹55,800. The number is computed deterministically: ₹67,000 repayment (2.5%/week × 52 weeks on ₹10,000) minus ₹11,200 (MUDRA Shishu at 12% APR) = ₹55,800 saved.

Agent vs. Pipeline Distinction — Why It Matters

True Agents (perceive → decide → act → adapt)

  • Sentinel: Perceives forwarded messages, decides risk via deterministic Trust Score, acts by surfacing alternatives, adapts as community reports grow.
  • Kalpa: Perceives user's financial context, decides math model (deterministic vs. P25/P50/P75 projection), acts by generating animated consequence timeline, adapts narrative to language and literacy level.
  • Prahari: Perceives user-reported events + uploaded statements, decides anomaly threshold, acts with proactive alerts, adapts intervention strategy based on user response history.

Pipeline Tools (input → deterministic output)

  • Drishti: OCR → field extraction → deterministic cost computation → nutrition label. No autonomy.
  • Vaani: Wraps Bhashini API for STT → NMT → TTS. Translation middleware, not an agent.
  • Praman: Logs every intervention, decision change, and outcome for impact measurement. A database writer, not an agent.

Calling a translation API wrapper an "agent" undermines the credibility of actual agentic components. This distinction signals architectural honesty.
